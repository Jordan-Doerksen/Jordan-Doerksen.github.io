Courier
Onboarding

Every delivery matters. Every courier is replaceable.

You pilot company hardware through increasingly populated airspace and complete each shift to standard. This manual teaches you how the job actually works — the flow of a shift, the systems you'll build with, and the management that arrives to end you. Read it in order; it's written to be read in order.

Standard is the minimum. The minimum is expected.

01

The one rule, first

There is no fire button.

Your guns aim and fire themselves. You never pull a trigger and you never manually aim the rack. Your job is three things: fly (where you sit decides what lives), draft (the cards you pick are your build), and point (focus-fire and target modes decide where the guns look). Internalize this one line and the rest of the game snaps into place.

The company finds manual triggers inefficient. The company finds most things inefficient.

02

The flow of a shift

One shift is a loop, and the loop is the whole game. You clock in, fly until something kills you, and file the paperwork — then you spend what you earned and clock in stronger. Death is payday, not failure.

▶ the shift loop — death routes you to the Depot, and back in richer
  • Clock in. The title runs a live demo of a maxed ship — that's what yours can become. Clocking in opens your contractor file (three save slots, three careers), then drops you in the yard.
  • The yard. A flyable company dock. The gantry swaps hulls, the platform is the Depot, and the black hole is the way out — flying into it is how a shift begins.
  • Sign the Pact. Crossing the black hole's ring opens the Hazard Pact: optionally toggle difficulty modifiers (more scrip + better rare odds) and any unlocked Directives, then START SHIFT.
  • Fly & farm. Waves escalate; kills drop XP gems that pull to you and fill the bar.
  • Level up → draft. The shift freezes on a four-card requisition. This screen is where your entire build happens.
  • Fight Supervisors. The first boss lands at ~150s, then roughly every ~130s. Your survival clock and scrip both freeze while a boss is alive — bosses are pure threat, paid by bounty.
  • Die, then read the review. The Performance Review itemizes the shift, banks your scrip, and sends you back to the yard — richer.

The meta loop in one line: survived time + boss kills → scrip → the Depot buys permanent power → the next shift runs longer → repeat. About 15 solid runs maxes the whole Depot board.

  • Boost — a short speed burst that drains a small fuel tank. It's a rhythm, not a hold: burn, coast, refill. Whole card families reward boosting (see Ship feel).

04

The run: waves & gems

The run is the space between boss fights, where the tenure clock actually runs. Enemies arrive in escalating waves; killing them is how you level, and leveling is how you build.

Gems are your XP

  • Kills drop gems that magnetize to you (magnet ~100px, collected at ~22px). Clustered gems coalesce into fewer chunky crystals, so a big kill isn't a cloud of pips to chase.
  • Any gem that drifts far past you auto-salvages and banks instantly — you never lose a drop, so don't chase a stray gem into danger.
  • The bar gets harder on a curve (roughly ×1.28 per level) with two real walls around levels 8 and 14 — early levels come fast, then slow.

Landmarks — points of interest

  • Structures drift into the shift — repair gantries, restocking points, buyback kiosks, wreck caches, and the rare unsanctioned vendor. Each pings the radar with its own icon; each does something real when you dock.
  • Docking is dwelling. No button — hold your ship inside the ring for about a second, under fire, and the service fires. One use each; the spent husk drifts off, and ignored ones expire on their own.
  • The vendor sells one off-schedule card at better rare odds — for scrip, or a hull pip if you can't pay. At your last pip and broke, it simply refuses. The company disapproves of the whole arrangement.

Inventory recovered from the void is company property. It was always company property.

05

The draft — where the build happens

Every level-up freezes the shift and deals a REQUISITION: four cards, pick one, resume. Each card shows a name, the real stat line (numbers, not vibes), and one deadpan description. Doing this dozens of times is your entire ship.

weapon
New / Another

Drops a gun into a free bay. "ANOTHER" is a second copy. Only offered while a bay is empty.

rank up
Upgrade

Mk I→II→III for an owned gun. Mk II switches on its element proc — a real spike.

run-wide
Passive

Permanent multipliers: fire rate, damage, pickup, speed, reach. They stack all run.

module
System

One-time ship modules, mostly boost/fuel-keyed. One of each per run.

discovered
Synergy

Appears only once you own both ingredients — a bonus for a specific pairing.

spend one
Reroll

Banked rerolls redraw the four cards. Save them for the drafts that matter.

Three drafts that hijack the requisition

  • FORK (guaranteed) — when an owned gun hits Mk III, your next draft is nothing but its two gold legendary finals. No reroll; you must pick one.
  • MERGER (M&A) — hold both named Mk★ halves of a recipe and the next draft leads with a MERGER card that fuses them into one super-weapon and frees a bay.
  • BOON — a boss kill opens a separate three-card boon pick. Some are Devil's Bargains: a bigger bonus with a themed cost.
06

Building your arsenal

Guns live in your hull's bays (the rack along the bottom). There are thirteen — nine that fire projectiles and four "system" bays that are their own kind of weapon. Each has a signature (a free perk it earns at rare) and a behaviour that decides how it teaches.

The nine guns

Pulse Array

Rapid light bolts that ricochet. Standard issue — like you.

Flak Spread

A short cone of pellets. Whatever gets close gets itemized.

Rail Lance

Slow, heavy, pierces the whole line. Hits like a freight bill.

Scatter Pods

Lobbed ordnance by the pallet; each pod bursts into fragments.

Needler

A thin, fast stinger that skewers several bodies.

Flame Jet Blaze

A hot cone; the burn spreads to neighbours.

Cryo Lance Frost

A piercing chill; a frozen enemy's death erupts in ice.

Tesla Coil Shock

Crackling darts whose chain leaps to more targets.

Void Bolt Void

A slow, heavy singularity. It arrives when it arrives.

The four system bays

  • Hangar Bay — launches a fighter wing (and a bomber at Mk V) that fights for you. Ranks to Mk V, not III.
  • Loss Prevention — a persistent damaging field around the ship. No aim, no ammo; anything close is processed.
  • Retention Policy — charged assets orbit you on a short leash; attrition is continuous and mandatory.
  • Solar Lance — a charging plasma beam that winds up, fires a burning burst, then recharges.
07

Tiers → Forks → Fusions

Every gun climbs the same ladder, and the top of it is the best feeling in the game.

▶ Mk I → the proc at Mk II → Mk III → the legendary fork → a fusion
  • Tiers (Mk I→III). UPGRADE cards rank a gun up. A duplicate draw of a gun you own auto-routes into a rank instead of a second mount.
  • Rarity & quality. Every card rolls a tier — CommonUncommonRareElite. Higher tiers add quality, and Elite guns bolt on a bespoke extra effect. Elite is a ~1% chase from minute zero.
  • Forks (Mk★). At Mk III a gun forks into one of two pre-authored legendaries — a permanent, screen-defining upgrade. That's the whole draft, guaranteed.
  • Fusions (M&A). Certain pairs of Mk★ legendaries can merge into a single over-the-top super-weapon that frees up a bay. Hold both halves and the merger comes to you.
08

The element wheel

Four elements ring a neutral Kinetic core. Each element beats one and is beaten by another — hit a weakness for ×1.5, hit a resistance for ×0.66. Kinetic never has a matchup; it's the reliable middle.

▶ the wheel — weakness ×1.5, resistance ×0.66, taught at the point of impact
  • A gun's element decides its proc, unlocked at Mk II: Blaze burns, Frost slows, Shock chains, Void pulls. Kinetic gets raw overpenetration instead.
  • You don't have to memorize the ring — the game shows the multiplier the instant a shot lands. But building around one element's proc is a real strategy.

Threats — the workforce & management

The workforce

  • Shape tells you the role. Enemy silhouettes are read by side-count; the more armored ones wear a shell over a weak core — strip the shell, then the core dies fast.
  • Champions & elites are bigger, meaner variants that drop more. As the shift ages, enemies shift into later "eras" — ringed, tougher stock.

Management (the bosses)

Bosses are the org chart, and they climb it. Each is a per-tier kit, and past tier one they flip into a nastier second phase at half health — more speed, minions, new attacks.

10

The three hulls

You unlock hulls at the Depot. Bay count is the real lever — how much firepower you can carry — and each hull plays a different fantasy.

Skirmisher · 6 bays · 1 pip
Courier

Quick, light, unloved. Speed is the health bar — one clean hit ends the shift, so don't get hit. Hot fire rate rewards staying in the fight.

Bruiser · 8 bays · 2 pips
Hauler

The wall of guns. Thin shield, the most bays — it wins by ending threats before they arrive. Every reload shoves the swarm back.

Commander · 3 bays · 2 pips
Carrier

A battle-group commander: your nose gun is a heavy rail that MARKS everything it pierces, and your air group hits marked targets at +50% and hunts them first. Its three exclusive bays — fighters, bombers, artillery frigates — rank to Mk IX, growing veteran crews, extra craft on a staggered cadence, and equipment (capped by ACE CREW). Shoot → mark → shred.

Pips are your hull's discrete health after the shield is down: a small ship has one, the carrier has three. Any hit with the shield down costs exactly one pip. Mistakes are counted, not amortized.

11

Scrip & the Depot

When a shift ends, your survived time and boss kills convert to scrip — company credit. You spend it at the Depot between runs on permanent upgrades. This is how you actually get stronger.

  • Tenure pays by the active minute — and remember the clock freezes during boss fights, so scrip rewards the time you spend surviving the swarm, plus a flat bounty per boss.
  • Depot riders are permanent, organized by department: bay leases (more slots), ordnance, plating, motor pool, logistics, payroll. The two better hulls are bought at the yard's drydock gantry.
  • The Performance Review keeps your records — best shift, lifetime totals — and those lifetime milestones quietly unlock the Directives below.

Scrip carries no cash value and expires upon separation. Efficiency is mandatory.

12

Playing on your own terms

Once the Depot's built out, three systems let you re-introduce challenge and variety on purpose — for better rewards.

The Hazard Pact

Before every shift you can toggle any combination of difficulty modifiers. Harder run → more scrip and better rare-card odds. It's a dial you control; leave it off to farm, crank it to chase.

Standing Directives

Five run-rewiring unlocks, each earned by a lifetime milestone (locked ones show redacted with their condition). They don't buff numbers — they change the rules, and any combination can be on at once:

  • Just-In-Time Delivery — never reload, but fire a little slower.
  • Zero Inventory — nothing drops; kills bank XP directly.
  • Hostile Workplace — shield removed, +2 pips, and contact injures the attacker.
  • Perpetual Motion Clause — boost never drains; standing still drains your shield.
  • Single Vendor Agreement — after your first install, the draft only ever offers that one weapon.

Degenerate combinations are permitted. Finding them is half the job.

Boons

Every boss kill also offers a three-card boon draft — run-wide effects in their own amber class. About half are Devil's Bargains: a bigger payoff carried with a themed downside. Take them when the run can afford the cost.

13

Ship feel & systems

A cluster of systems all key off one thing: your boost. Master the burn-and-coast rhythm and a whole card family pays off.

  • The boost rhythm. Boost is a small tank you spend and refill. System cards build on it — Vapor Recovery (a knockback burst when the tank runs dry), Full Burn (more damage while boosting), Refuel Rebate (a shield chip when it re-arms).
  • The Near-Miss Clause (dodge). Certain riders give you a chance for an incoming hit to simply whiff — a dodged shot costs you nothing and grants a sliver of grace. Flying toward danger is safer than it looks with dodge stacked.
  • The prow. Every hull has a fixed nose gun that auto-ranks on its own as you clear waves — free baseline damage you never have to draft.
  • Jet Wash & friends. Later systems lay a burning wake behind a boosting ship, patch a hull pip on a boss kill, or add plating on the spot. Read the stat line; the whisper of voice is just garnish.

Colophon

Same seed, same shift. Fulfillment is deterministic — the same starting conditions produce the same run, every time. The company does not believe in luck. Only in variance it has not yet priced.
